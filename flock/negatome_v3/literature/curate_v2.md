You are curating candidate non-interacting protein pairs for a benchmark dataset (project "Flock"). Each pair was flagged by an automated screen as a paper reporting that two proteins do NOT interact, and each side was then mapped to a UniProt accession automatically — by name matching, by a species rule, or by a model choosing from candidates. Both the interaction claim and the accessions are under test.

For EVERY pair in the packet, decide:

1. `mapping_verdict` — is the recorded accession actually the protein this paper is talking about? One of `correct`, `wrong_paralog` (right family, wrong member), `wrong_protein` (a different protein entirely, often a gene symbol collision), `wrong_species` (right protein, an organism the paper is not studying), `fragment_or_isoform_mismatch` (the card length or isoform cannot be the paper's protein), `affinity_tag_or_reporter` (the partner is really GST/GFP/His from the pull-down), or `cannot_determine`. Check BOTH sides, using organism, gene names, protein names and length on the cards. Pay attention to `how_accession_a_was_chosen`: where `species_source` is `no_species_check_ran`, nothing has verified that the organism matches the paper, and where `assignment` is `model_picked`, another model chose that accession from a list of candidates and was right 85-87% of the time — treat it as a proposal to check against the paper rather than as settled.
2. `relationship` — `non_interaction`, `positive_interaction`, `both_reported` (genuinely both, e.g. binding under one condition and not another), `not_discussed`, or `no_evidence` (mentioned, no claim either way).
3. `evidence_sentence` — the single best VERBATIM sentence from the paper text supporting your `relationship` call. It must appear in the text exactly; copy it, do not paraphrase. Empty string if none.
4. `evidence_location` — one of abstract, introduction, results, discussion, results_and_discussion, figure_caption, table_caption, supplementary, other, none.
5. `qualifier` — what the negation is actually about. One of `full_length_pair`, `domain_fragment_only`, `mutant_only`, `different_paralog`, `cited_prior_work`, `not_a_protein_pair`, `indirect_or_functional_only`, or `unclear`. A negation about a truncation, a point mutant or a different family member does NOT establish that the recorded full-length pair fails to interact. Two further cases to watch for, both of which look like clean negatives until you read what is being denied:
   - `not_a_protein_pair` — what the paper says one protein does not bind is not a protein at all: a promoter or other DNA region, an RNA, a metabolite, a lipid or a drug. Use this even when both accessions name real proteins and are correctly mapped. "VerZ does not interact with the promoter region of verJ" is a protein-DNA claim, not a protein-protein one.
   - `indirect_or_functional_only` — the negative result is a knockdown, depletion, localisation, genetic or phenotypic observation rather than a test of whether the two proteins bind each other. That depleting one protein does not change where another one localises is not evidence that the two do not interact.
6. `usable_as_negative` — true only if mapping is correct on both sides AND relationship is `non_interaction` AND qualifier is `full_length_pair` AND nothing anywhere in the paper contradicts it.
7. `confidence` — high, medium or low.
8. `reasoning` — one to three sentences, naming the section you relied on and being concrete about why.

A standing ruling that overrides the obvious reading: an association mediated by a third protein is NOT an interaction for this benchmark. Where a paper shows no direct A-B interaction and the only positive it reports is bridged through some other protein, the pair is a genuine negative — `non_interaction`, not `both_reported`. This is about a positive the paper reports, and does not license the reverse: an indirect negative result is still `indirect_or_functional_only`.

Emit a JSON array, one object per pair, with exactly these keys: pmid, pair, mapping_verdict, mapping_note, relationship, evidence_sentence, evidence_location, qualifier, usable_as_negative, confidence, reasoning. `pair` must be the exact `pair` string from the packet. `mapping_note` names the protein you think the accession should be, or "" when mapping is correct. Cover every pair — do not skip any.
